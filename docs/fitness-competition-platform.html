Fitness Competition Platform

Full-Stack Architecture, Mobile Development, and Accelerated Simulation

The Project

I designed and developed a mobile fitness competition platform intended to encourage regular physical activity through time-based challenges and rewards.

Participants could join competitions, track their activity, manage competing priorities, and work toward completion requirements. The application needed to coordinate user activity, competition deadlines, participation limits, and reward-related workflows.

I was responsible for the entire technical implementation, including the mobile application, backend services, database, and supporting development infrastructure.

Architecture

The system consisted of a React Native mobile application, a TypeScript/Express REST API, and a PostgreSQL database.

The mobile application provided competition discovery, enrollment, participation tracking, check-ins, and reward-related interfaces. The backend managed authentication, business rules, persistence, and scheduled processing.

One interesting aspect of the architecture was separating competitions from individual participation. A single workout check-in could contribute toward multiple competitions, with participants controlling which active competitions received credit through a prioritized list.

The data model maintained distinct records for competitions, participation, and check-in events. Background processing handled competition expiration and participation outcomes.

The development environment used Docker, Kubernetes, and Helm.

The Interesting Challenge: Testing Time

Competitions were designed to unfold over multiple days. Waiting for real-world deadlines during development would have made testing and iteration unnecessarily slow.

I addressed this by introducing an accelerated development mode.

Instead of requiring daily check-ins and waiting days for competitions to expire, the development environment could use minute-based check-in intervals and short competition durations.

I also developed a simulation that generated synthetic participants, created competitions, and produced recurring activity using the application's underlying business logic.

This allowed the environment to continually generate changing conditions, including enrollment, participation, completion, and expiration.

Importantly, the simulation operated alongside the actual application.

Testers and stakeholders could use the mobile interface while simulated users and competitions progressed in the background. This made it possible to explore interactions and observe application behavior across multiple competition lifecycles without waiting through real-world schedules.

The simulation was a development and testing tool, not a substitute for comprehensive automated testing or a claim of production-scale load testing.

Engineering Considerations

Several aspects of the project required more than conventional application development.

Competition participation involved time-based eligibility, capacity constraints, priorities, and lifecycle transitions. These rules needed to remain consistent across mobile interactions, backend operations, and scheduled processing.

The architecture also needed to support meaningful testing of stateful behavior. Rather than relying exclusively on static test records, the development environment could continually produce evolving application data.

I favored a relatively straightforward application architecture, with a single backend and background processors, rather than introducing distributed services before they were justified.

The result was an environment that supported both development and interactive product demonstrations.

Outcome

The project reached a functional prototype with a mobile application, backend services, persistent data, and a working development environment.

Testers and stakeholders interacted with the application, including while the accelerated simulation was running.

Although the product was not commercially launched during my involvement, the project demonstrated end-to-end product engineering, relational data modeling, time-based workflow design, mobile development, and practical approaches to testing complex application behavior.

It was also an entirely hands-on implementation, completed before I adopted the agentic development practices I use today.
